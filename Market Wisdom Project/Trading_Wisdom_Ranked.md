# Trading & Investing Wisdom: Ranked by Consensus Across 727 Books

Sep 29, 2026 · @Chris

## Method

Trend following tops the list: 221 of the 560 books with usable content teach it. Stop-losses (203) and emotional control (196) come next.

- **Corpus:** 727 .txt files were scanned in 40 batches. 560 contained at least one strategy; the rest were empty, garbled or off-topic.
- **Extraction:** each file's strategies were named against a shared taxonomy, counted once per file.
- **Merging:** 443 raw strategy names were merged into 108 strategies. Near-duplicates (for example 13 Elliott Wave variants, 15 candlestick patterns) were folded into one entry.
- **Ranking:** by the number of distinct books that mention a strategy, not by how often it is repeated.

Counts below are books out of 560. A book counts once per entry, even if it matched several merged variants.

## Tier 1: Near-universal (100+ books)

- **#1 Follow the Trend / Trade With the Trend** · 221 books. Take positions in the direction of the prevailing trend rather than fighting it. "The trend is your friend" is the single most repeated rule in the corpus.
- **#2 Always Use a Stop-Loss** · 203 books. Set a price where you exit a losing trade before you enter it. It caps the damage from any single mistake.
- **#3 Control Emotions: Avoid Fear and Greed** · 196 books. Fear makes traders exit winners early and greed makes them hold or oversize. Mechanical rules and self-awareness keep both in check.
- **#4 Follow Your Trading Plan Without Deviation** · 188 books. Plan the trade, then trade the plan. Deviating mid-trade turns a tested system into guesswork.
- **#5 Support and Resistance Levels** · 179 books. Prices tend to stall at levels where buyers or sellers stepped in before. Those levels frame entries, stops and targets.
- **#6 Momentum Indicators (RSI, MACD, Stochastics)** · 175 books. Oscillators gauge the speed of price moves and flag overbought or oversold conditions.
- **#7 Cut Losses Short, Let Winners Run** · 159 books. Small losses and large gains make a system profitable even with a win rate under 50%.
- **#8 Use Moving Averages to Identify Trend** · 153 books. Price above a rising average signals an uptrend; crossovers of fast and slow averages mark changes.
- **#9 Position Sizing Based on Risk** · 131 books. Size each position by the distance to your stop and the capital at risk, not by conviction.
- **#10 Breakout Trading** · 127 books. Enter when price clears resistance or breaks support on strength, and filter out false breakouts.
- **#11 Have a Predefined Exit Plan** · 126 books. Know your profit target and your stop before entry, so exits aren't decided under pressure.
- **#12 Continuous Education and Self-Improvement** · 116 books. Markets change; successful traders keep studying, reviewing mistakes and refining skills.
- **#13 Avoid Overleveraging / Excessive Margin** · 111 books. Leverage magnifies losses as much as gains and is the most common cause of blown accounts.
- **#14 Chart Patterns** · 106 books. Head-and-shoulders, triangles, flags and channels signal likely continuation or reversal.
- **#15 Discipline Beats Intelligence** · 106 books. Consistent execution of simple rules outperforms clever analysis applied erratically.
- **#16 Risk-Reward Ratio Before Entering** · 106 books. Only take trades where the potential gain is a multiple of the risk, and know the system's expectancy.
- **#17 Backtest Before Using Real Money** · 104 books. Test a system on historical data, including its maximum drawdown, before committing capital.
- **#18 Diversify Across Uncorrelated Assets** · 103 books. Holding assets that don't move together reduces portfolio volatility without equally reducing return.
- **#19 Patience: Wait for High-Probability Setups** · 101 books. Most of trading is waiting. When in doubt, stay out.

## Tier 2: Widely shared (50–99 books)

- **#20 Fibonacci Retracement Levels** · 94 books. Pullbacks often stall near 38.2%, 50% or 61.8% of the prior move, which gives candidate entries and targets.
- **#21 Liquidity Matters When Entering and Exiting** · 94 books. Thinly traded markets mean wide spreads and slippage; trade where you can get in and out cleanly.
- **#22 Markets Are Cyclical** · 92 books. Bull and bear phases recur with the business cycle and crowd psychology.
- **#23 Understand Implied Volatility Before Trading Options** · 92 books. An option's price depends heavily on expected volatility; buying when it is high can lose money even if direction is right.
- **#24 Volume Confirms Price Action** · 91 books. Moves on rising volume are more trustworthy; accumulation and distribution show up in volume first.
- **#25 Keep a Trading Journal** · 89 books. Recording every trade and your reasoning exposes recurring mistakes and real edge.
- **#26 Manage Expectations: Trading Is Not Gambling** · 87 books. Aim for consistent modest returns and treat get-rich-quick claims as warning signs.
- **#27 Never Risk More Than 1–2% Per Trade** · 87 books. A fixed small risk per trade lets you survive long losing streaks.
- **#28 Long-Term Investing Beats Market Timing** · 85 books. Staying invested usually beats trying to call tops and bottoms, which few do reliably.
- **#29 Currencies Move on Rate Differentials and News** · 83 books. Forex prices respond to interest-rate gaps, carry, central-bank action and data surprises.
- **#30 Keep Trading Systems Simple** · 81 books. Few rules and few indicators hold up better out of sample; there is no holy-grail indicator.
- **#31 Candlestick Patterns** · 79 books. Dojis, hammers, engulfing and star patterns signal short-term reversals or continuation.
- **#32 Interest Rates Affect Asset Prices** · 79 books. Rising rates pressure bond and stock valuations; falling rates support them.
- **#33 Hedge With Options or Futures** · 78 books. Protective puts, collars and offsetting futures limit downside on existing holdings.
- **#34 Use Multiple Timeframes for Confirmation** · 77 books. Check the higher timeframe's trend before acting on a signal from a shorter one.
- **#35 Preserve Capital First, Profit Second** · 75 books. Only risk money you can afford to lose; staying in the game matters more than any single gain.
- **#36 Don't Let Ego Affect Decisions** · 72 books. Take responsibility, admit when you're wrong and don't marry a position.
- **#37 Contrarian Investing** · 65 books. Be fearful when others are greedy; crowd extremes and manias often mark turning points.
- **#38 Analyze Financial Statements** · 63 books. Read the income statement, balance sheet and cash flows before buying a company.
- **#39 Supply and Demand Drive Price** · 62 books. Every price move reflects an imbalance between buyers and sellers.
- **#40 Buy Low, Sell High (Value Investing)** · 61 books. Buy assets below their intrinsic value; a cheap price alone is not value.
- **#41 Accept Losses as a Cost of Business** · 60 books. Losses are inevitable and expected; treating them as failures leads to worse decisions.
- **#42 Adapt Strategy to Market Conditions** · 60 books. Trending, ranging and volatile markets reward different approaches; filter for regime first.
- **#43 Diversify Across Asset Classes** · 60 books. Spread capital across stocks, bonds, cash and others, and rebalance to target weights.
- **#44 Avoid Overtrading** · 58 books. Too many trades or markets raise costs and lower quality; take breaks.
- **#45 Earnings Growth Drives Stock Price** · 57 books. Over time, share prices follow the growth of company earnings.
- **#46 Assess Management and Competitive Moat** · 56 books. Durable advantages and capable, honest management protect long-term returns.
- **#47 Don't Average Down on Losers** · 55 books. Adding to a losing position compounds the mistake instead of fixing it.
- **#48 Invest in Companies You Understand** · 51 books. Stay within your circle of competence so you can judge the business.
- **#49 Compound Interest / Reinvest Returns** · 50 books. Reinvested gains grow exponentially; time in the market is the key input.

## Tier 3: Specialist (10–49 books)

- **#50 Time Decay (Theta) Erodes Option Value** · 42 books. Options lose value each day, faster near expiry, which favors sellers over time.
- **#51 Avoid Revenge Trading** · 37 books. Trying to win back a loss right away leads to oversized, unplanned trades.
- **#52 Set Clear Financial Goals** · 36 books. Know why you invest or trade and what you are aiming for.
- **#53 Live Below Your Means / Save Consistently** · 34 books. Steady saving builds the capital that investing then compounds.
- **#54 Dollar-Cost Averaging** · 31 books. Invest a fixed amount at regular intervals to smooth out entry prices.
- **#55 Margin of Safety** · 26 books. Buy well below estimated value so errors in your estimate don't cause losses.
- **#56 Avoid High-Interest Consumer Debt** · 21 books. Paying off expensive debt is a guaranteed return few investments beat.
- **#57 Elliott Wave Analysis** · 18 books. Markets move in five-wave impulses and three-wave corrections, which some use to forecast turns.
- **#58 Build an Emergency Fund** · 15 books. Cash reserves prevent forced selling at bad times.
- **#59 Avoid Curve-Fitting a System** · 12 books. Over-optimized backtests fit past noise and fail live; distrust unverified results.
- **#60 Pay Yourself First** · 11 books. Save before spending, not from what is left over.
- **#61 Seasonal and Calendar Patterns** · 10 books. Effects like "Sell in May," the January effect and the presidential cycle show recurring tendencies.
- **#62 Use Trailing Stops** · 10 books. Move the stop with price to lock in gains while giving the trade room.

## Tier 4: Niche (2–9 books)

- **#63 Be Skeptical of Gurus, Tips and Hype** · 9 books. Think independently; tips, newsletters and brokers often have conflicts.
- **#64 Match Your Method to Your Personality** · 9 books. A system you can't follow comfortably will be abandoned at the worst moment.
- **#65 Understand and Manage Credit Scores** · 9 books. Good credit lowers borrowing costs.
- **#66 Market Breadth and Sentiment Indicators** · 7 books. Advance-decline lines, put/call ratios and the VIX confirm trends or flag extremes.
- **#67 Pyramid Into Winning Positions** · 7 books. Add to winners in smaller increments, never to losers.
- **#68 Quantify Portfolio Risk** · 7 books. Use standard deviation, value-at-risk and stress tests to measure exposure.
- **#69 Understand Order Types; Prefer Limit Orders** · 7 books. Order choice and routing affect fill prices and costs.
- **#70 Arbitrage and Market-Neutral Strategies** · 6 books. Pairs, merger and convertible arbitrage profit from price gaps between related assets.
- **#71 Bollinger Bands** · 6 books. Volatility bands flag stretched prices and volatility squeezes.
- **#72 Follow Insider and Institutional Activity** · 6 books. Insider buying, buybacks and commercial positioning (COT) hint at informed views.
- **#73 Kelly Criterion / Optimal f** · 6 books. Formulas that size bets by edge and odds to maximize long-run growth.
- **#74 Price Action Over Indicators; Seek Confirmation** · 6 books. Require several tools to agree rather than trusting one signal.
- **#75 Scale In and Out of Positions** · 6 books. Enter and take profits in parts to reduce timing risk.
- **#76 Sector Rotation With the Economic Cycle** · 6 books. Different sectors lead at different cycle stages; analyze top-down.
- **#77 Price–Indicator Divergence Signals Reversal** · 5 books. When price makes a new high but momentum doesn't, the trend may be tiring.
- **#78 Minimize Fees, Costs and Taxes** · 5 books. Costs compound against you as surely as returns compound for you.
- **#79 Avoid Trading Around High-Volatility News** · 4 books. Releases cause erratic spikes; "buy the rumor, sell the news."
- **#80 Concentrate in High-Conviction Ideas** · 4 books. Some argue few well-researched positions beat over-diversification.
- **#81 Guard Against Cognitive Biases** · 4 books. Overconfidence, anchoring, hindsight and loss aversion distort decisions.
- **#82 Know and Define Your Trading Edge** · 4 books. Be able to state why your setup should make money.
- **#83 Avoid Choppy, Range-Bound Markets** · 3 books. Trend systems bleed in sideways markets.
- **#84 Reflexivity: Markets Can Stay Irrational** · 3 books. Perceptions feed back into fundamentals, and mispricing can outlast your capital.
- **#85 Relative Strength: Buy Market Leaders** · 3 books. Stocks outperforming the market tend to keep doing so.
- **#86 Tactical Asset Allocation on Valuation** · 3 books. Shift allocations based on valuation or index timing signals.
- **#87 Time Trades to the Best Sessions** · 3 books. Liquidity and movement concentrate in certain hours.
- **#88 Treat Each Trade as Independent** · 3 books. Past outcomes don't change the next trade's odds; avoid martingale doubling.
- **#89 Evaluate Fund Managers Properly** · 2 books. Use time-weighted total returns and due diligence when choosing managers.
- **#90 Maintain Physical and Mental Health** · 2 books. Sleep, exercise and stress control support decision quality.
- **#91 Mean Reversion and Fading Moves** · 2 books. Bet on stretched prices returning toward the average.
- **#92 Practice With Paper Trading First** · 2 books. Use a demo account before risking real money.
- **#93 Price Gaps and Gap Trading** · 2 books. Gaps often fill, or signal continuation or exhaustion.
- **#94 Short Selling** · 2 books. Profit from falling prices by selling borrowed shares.
- **#95 Swing Trading** · 2 books. Hold positions for days to capture short-term swings.
- **#96 Valuation Models (DCF, Real Options)** · 2 books. Value a business on discounted future cash flows and the worth of its flexibility.

## Tier 5: Single-book ideas (1 book each)

These 12 entries (ranks 97–108) each appear in one book only. Most are proprietary systems or narrow techniques.

- **Acting in Your Own Best Interest**: a trading-psychology principle about self-sabotage.
- **Automated Trading Systems**: letting code execute rules to remove discretion.
- **Avoid Trading in Contract Delivery Months**: futures liquidity and delivery risk rise near expiry.
- **Basic Options Strategies**: long calls and puts, covered writes, and bull and bear spreads.
- **Day Trading Systems**: intraday rule sets.
- **E.A.S.Y Method**: a named proprietary entry system.
- **Ladder Bond Maturities**: stagger maturities to manage reinvestment risk.
- **Market Profile and Value-Area Analysis**: reading where volume clusters by price.
- **Master Trader Principles**: a book-specific rule set.
- **Small-Cap Premium May Be Illusory**: the size premium can vanish after costs and liquidity.
- **Special Situations: Reverse Mergers and Shell Companies**: event-driven plays on corporate restructurings.
- **Tradetables System**: a named proprietary system.
